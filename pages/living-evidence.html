Interpreting the Findings
A broader view of psychological wellbeing
Psychological wellbeing is multidimensional. Improvements in one domain do not always occur alongside improvements in another. Someone may think more clearly while still feeling emotionally overwhelmed, or regain physical calm before experiencing greater motivation or purpose. Looking at these dimensions together provides a more balanced understanding of overall mental wellbeing than relying on a single measure alone.
Mood Quotient is designed as a composite observational measure that integrates five independently assessed dimensions into a single summary score. The individual dimensions remain visible because each reflects a distinct aspect of psychological function and may respond differently across participants.
Methodological Note
Values shown represent the average percentage improvement observed among participants with paired pre- and post-programme assessments available for each dimension. Participant counts may differ across measures because incomplete assessment pairs are excluded from individual calculations.

Interpreting the Findings
Looking beyond individual biomarkers
Human biology functions as an interconnected network of physiological systems. While individual biomarkers provide valuable clinical information, they often describe only one aspect of health. Changes observed in one marker may occur independently of improvements elsewhere, making isolated values difficult to interpret in the context of whole-body wellbeing.
Bio-Vitality brings together six independently evaluated physiological systems into a single composite measure. Each system reflects a different aspect of biological function and remains visible alongside the overall score, allowing improvements to be understood both individually and collectively.
Methodological Note
Average percentage improvements are calculated from paired participant assessments for each physiological system. Because laboratory investigations and clinical measurements were not available for every participant, the number of participants differs between systems.

Living Evidence
Understanding the Results
Living Evidence is an observational framework that summarises biological and psychological changes measured before and after participation. Rather than focusing on a single laboratory value, it presents multiple dimensions of health together to provide a broader picture of human wellbeing.
How are averages calculated?
Average improvements are calculated using paired measurements collected before and after participation. Each participant contributes equally to the average regardless of their starting value.
What does "Highest observed" mean?
Highest observed values represent the largest individual improvements recorded within the current Living Evidence dataset. They illustrate the range of changes observed and should not be interpreted as expected outcomes for every participant.
Why do participant numbers differ?
Not every participant completed every laboratory investigation or assessment. Consequently, the number of paired observations may vary between biomarkers.
Why are some values shown as percentages?
Percentage change allows biomarkers measured in different units to be compared more intuitively. Measures such as blood pressure, body weight and biological age are presented in their original clinical units because those units are widely understood and clinically meaningful.
Scientific note
Living Evidence summarises observational outcomes from participants within the Moodforest programme. The information presented is intended to improve transparency and understanding of observed biological changes and should not be interpreted as evidence of clinical efficacy or as medical advice.
Living Evidence evolves continuously as additional participant observations become available. All summaries shown in this report reflect the dataset available at the time of publication.
